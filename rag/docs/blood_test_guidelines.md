# Blood Test Preparation Guide: Dos and Don'ts

Properly preparing for a blood test ensures that your lab results are accurate and reliable. Depending on the specific panel ordered by your healthcare provider (e.g., Fasting Blood Glucose, Lipid Panel, Metabolic Panel, Hormone Panel), certain factors like diet, hydration, exercise, and medication can impact your numbers.

---

## 🟢 The "DOs" Before a Blood Test

### 1. **Do Confirm Fasting Requirements**
* **Why:** Certain tests—such as fasting blood sugar, lipid panels (cholesterol), and comprehensive metabolic panels—require you to abstain from food and drink for a specified period.
* **Action:** Ask your doctor or the lab if fasting is necessary. Fasting usually lasts **8 to 12 hours** prior to the draw.

### 2. **Do Stay Well-Hydrated with Plain Water**
* **Why:** Water increases blood volume and plumps up your veins, making the phlebotomist's job easier and reducing the chance of repeated needle sticks. Hydration also prevents false elevations in concentration-sensitive markers like blood urea nitrogen (BUN).
* **Action:** Drink plenty of plain water the day before and the morning of your test (even if you are fasting from food).

### 3. **Do Inform the Lab About Medications & Supplements**
* **Why:** Prescription drugs, over-the-counter medications, vitamins, and herbal supplements can interfere with specific biochemical assays.
* **Action:** Provide a complete list of everything you take to the technician. Ask your doctor beforehand if you should take your daily morning medications before or after the blood draw.

### 4. **Do Get Adequate Rest**
* **Why:** Lack of sleep and high physiological stress can elevate stress hormones like cortisol and adrenaline, which in turn can temporary skew glucose levels and liver enzyme readings.
* **Action:** Aim for 7 to 9 hours of quality sleep the night before your test.

### 5. **Do Wear Accessible Clothing**
* **Why:** Easy access to your inner elbow (antecubital fossa) makes the process smoother and faster.
* **Action:** Wear short sleeves or a shirt with loose sleeves that roll up easily above the elbow.

## 🔴 The "DON'Ts" Before a Blood Test

### 1. **Don't Eat or Drink Anything (Except Water) If Fasting**
* **Why:** Calories, fats, sugars, and even artificial sweeteners trigger metabolic processes. Coffee, tea, juices, and milk can alter glucose, insulin, liver, and triglyceride readings.
* **Action:** Avoid all foods, beverages, candy, and chewing gum during your designated fasting window.

### 2. **Don't Drink Alcohol for 24–48 Hours**
* **Why:** Alcohol consumption can alter blood sugar levels, liver enzymes (such as GGT and ALT), and triglyceride levels.
* **Action:** Avoid alcoholic beverages for at least 24 hours prior to blood collection.

### 3. **Don't Engage in Strenuous Exercise**
* **Why:** Heavy lifting or intense cardiovascular workouts can temporarily raise muscle enzymes (e.g., Creatine Kinase), alter electrolyte balances, and impact liver function markers.
* **Action:** Stick to light activity or rest the day before and the morning of your test.

### 4. **Don't Smoke or Use Nicotine**
* **Why:** Nicotine triggers adrenaline release, elevates blood pressure, alters cortisol levels, and can affect glucose levels.
* **Action:** Refrain from smoking or using nicotine products for at least 1 hour (ideally longer) before your blood test.

### 5. **Don't Take Biotin (Vitamin B7) Without Checking**
* **Why:** High doses of biotin supplements (commonly found in hair, skin, and nail formulations) can cause falsely high or low readings in thyroid tests (TSH, Free T3/T4) and cardiac markers like troponin.
* **Action:** Consult your provider about pausing biotin supplements 48 to 72 hours before testing.

## 📋 Quick Reference Checklist

| Category | Do | Don't |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hydration** | Drink plenty of plain water | Drink coffee, tea, juice, or soda while fasting |
| **Diet** | Follow 8–12 hr fast if instructed | Eat snacks, chew gum, or mints during fasting |
| **Activity** | Rest and keep stress low | Perform heavy weightlifting or intense cardio |
| **Substances** | Take necessary meds if approved | Consume alcohol (24h) or smoke/vape prior |
| **Supplements** | Tell your lab tech what you take | Take high-dose Biotin without consulting |
